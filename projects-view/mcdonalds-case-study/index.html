McDonald's Nutritional Diagnostic Report

Overview

What this project is about

Fast food chains generate large volumes of nutritional data across hundreds of menu items, but without structure that data rarely tells a clear health story. This project takes a real multi-chain fast food dataset — covering eight restaurants, 515 total items, and 17 nutritional attributes — and focuses it on McDonald's to surface meaningful patterns.

The goal was to diagnose McDonald's menu nutritionally: which categories carry the most risk, where does sodium become dangerous, how does the menu compare to competitors, and what does the "healthy" segment actually look like? Every query was designed to answer a specific question, and the dashboard was built to make those answers visible at a glance.

Phase 01

SQL Analysis in MySQL

Before any dashboard, the data had to be interrogated at the query level. Using MySQL Workbench and a live remote database connection, I worked through ten structured analytical queries — each designed to pull a specific nutritional insight from the dataset. The queries built a layered picture, from simple aggregations to category-level and cross-chain comparisons.

Precision mattered at every step. Working with columns like cal_fat and total_fat — which look similar but represent entirely different things — required carefully deciding what was actually being measured before writing a single line.

Q01
Total number of items and unique restaurant chains in the dataset
Q02
Average calorie count per restaurant across all eight chains
Q03

Q04
Nutritional breakdown by food category — chicken, burgers, and salads

Q07

Phase 02

Diagnostic Findings

Five critical findings emerged from the analysis — each backed by both SQL queries and Power BI visuals. Together they build a complete nutritional picture of the McDonald's menu.

F01
High Risk
Menu Category Risk Profile
Chicken = 54% of menu · 774 kcal avg
Three categories tell very different stories. Chicken items dominate the menu at 31 of 57 items and carry the heaviest nutritional load — 2.3× the sodium and 1.7× the calories of salads. Burgers sit in a moderate middle ground, while salads represent the only consistently lower-risk category.

F02
Distribution
Calorie Distribution Across the Menu
42% cluster in the 300–600 kcal range
The majority of McDonald's menu sits in the mid-calorie 400–700 kcal band. But 28% of items exceed 700 kcal, and a high-risk 14% breach 900 kcal — all of which are chicken or nugget-based items. Only 16% sit under 300 kcal, mostly salads and small McNugget portions.
F03

Sodium — The Primary Health Concern
74% of items exceed 1,000mg sodium
Sodium is where McDonald's menu becomes genuinely alarming. The average item delivers 62% of the adult recommended daily intake. 42 of 57 items are flagged as high-risk, and the single worst offender delivers 6,080mg — 2.6× the entire daily limit in one order.

Phase 03

Power BI Dashboard

With the SQL analysis complete, the insights were brought to life in Power BI via a live ODBC connection to MySQL. DAX measures were written to calculate calorie band classification, sodium risk flags, protein ratios, and healthy item thresholds. The result is a fully interactive report.

Dashboard Architecture

📦 Six KPI Cards
57 Total Items · 640 kcal Avg Calorie · 32g Avg Fat · 11 Better Choices · 40g Avg Protein · 1K Avg Sodium. All live-calculated via DAX measures.
🍩 CalorieBand Donut Chart
Three bands — ≤400, 401–700, and >700 kcal — give an instant summary. The 401–700 band is the dominant slice.
📊 High Calories Bar Chart
Top calorie offenders ranked. 20pc Crispy Tenders lead at 2.4K kcal. All top items are chicken-based without exception.
⚠️ High Sodium Bar Chart
Sodium rankings closely mirror calories. 20pc Crispy Tenders top both at 6.1K mg. The same items are the biggest red flags on the menu.
📈 Avg Nutrition by Category
Tenders have the worst ratio: 69.9% sodium share but only 26.8% protein ratio. Burgers are the most efficient at 34.7% protein share.
🔘 Healthy / Unhealthy Filters
Toggle buttons filter the entire page by health classification. An item-level checkbox slicer allows drill-down to specific items.
Insights

What the data revealed

McDonald's is the Calorie Leader
At 640 kcal per item average, McDonald's sits at the top across all eight chains — 67% higher than Chick Fil-A's 384 kcal.
Sodium is the Primary Risk Factor
Average sodium per item is 1,438mg — 62% of the recommended daily limit. 74% of items exceed 1,000mg.
Chicken Drives Both Volume and Risk
Chicken is 54% of the menu yet averages 2.3× the sodium and 1.7× the calories of salads.
Salads are the Only Consistently Safe Category
Salads average 332 kcal and 824mg sodium. Only 11 of 57 items pass the dual threshold.
Burgers Have the Best Protein Efficiency
Burgers offer the best protein-to-sodium ratio (34.72% vs 61.55%). Tenders are the inverse.
Calorie Distribution Skews Mid-to-High
42% cluster in 400–700 kcal, 28% exceed 700 kcal, and 14% breach 900 kcal. Only 16% sit under 300 kcal.

Reflection

What this project taught me

🔍
Column names don't tell the whole story
Working with a raw nutritional dataset means confronting columns like cal_fat and total_fat that look similar but represent entirely different things. Deciding which to use before writing a single query is a key instinct.
🔗
Live connections change how you think about reports
Setting up an ODBC connection from MySQL to Power BI meant the dashboard wasn't a static screenshot — it was a live view into the database. That shift changes how you design both layers.
📊
The insight only lands when the framing is right
Knowing the 20pc Crispy Tenders contain 6,080mg of sodium is a raw fact. Saying it's 2.6× the entire recommended daily limit is an insight. Building that context into DAX measures is where analysis becomes communication.
